# Success Definition

## Purpose

Define campaign success and failure using verifiable system behavior.

## Scope

This definition establishes categories; detailed thresholds belong to future KPI
and review sprints.

## Theory

Success requires both product evidence and process integrity. External responses
can inform strategy but cannot be acceptance criteria for a learner-controlled
system.

## Framework

Success requires:

1. The campaign is operated through documented plans and reviews.
2. Claims of competence link to assessments or artifacts.
3. At least one coherent technical portfolio path is completed and verified.
4. Priority foundation gaps are reassessed.
5. Placement, research, and communication evidence is organized.
6. Safety, privacy, ethics, and recovery constraints are observed.
7. A final review records limitations and next-cycle decisions.

Failure is declared when evidence is falsified, critical safety boundaries are
knowingly violated, required reviews are abandoned without recovery, or the
campaign is claimed complete without exit evidence. A missed target or rejected
application alone is not system failure.

## Implementation

At each phase gate, classify criteria as `met`, `partially met`, `not met`, or
`not assessable`, and cite evidence. Never backfill unsupported results.

## Checklist

- [ ] Product and process evidence are both reviewed.
- [ ] External outcomes remain contextual.
- [ ] Limitations are disclosed.

## Decision Framework

Complete only when all mandatory exit criteria are met. Otherwise extend,
conclude as incomplete with an honest report, or authorize a new cycle.

## Common Failure Modes

- Moving criteria after results are known.
- Counting hours without evidence quality.
- Hiding failed experiments.

## Recovery Strategy

Restore the original criteria, validate evidence, and issue a corrective record.

## Examples

An unsuccessful interview with a completed review can strengthen the system. An
offer obtained without the required evidence does not prove campaign completion.
