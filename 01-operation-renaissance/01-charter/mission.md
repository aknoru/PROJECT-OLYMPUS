# Operation Renaissance Mission

## Purpose

Define the campaign assignment and distinguish it from vision, objectives,
criteria, failure conditions, and constraints.

## Scope

Operation Renaissance is a reusable 184-day cycle from August 1 through January
31. The year is configuration, not part of this charter.

## Theory

A mission states what the system must accomplish within its boundary. It should
be stable enough to guide trade-offs while leaving implementation to downstream
plans.

## Framework

**Mission:** During 184 days, establish and operate an evidence-producing
engineering system that strengthens foundations, verifies technical capability,
and prepares reusable evidence for examination, placement, projects, research,
communication, financial literacy, and sustainable performance.

**Vision:** the longer-horizon state described in [Vision](vision.md).

**Objectives:** measurable areas of effort in [Campaign Objectives](objectives.md).

**Success criteria:** verified completion conditions in
[Success Definition](success-model.md).

**Failure criteria:** unsafe operation, falsified evidence, abandonment of review,
or completion claims without required evidence.

**Constraints:** capacity, safety, time, resources, privacy, and ethics defined in
[Constraints](constraints.md).

## Implementation

Use the mission to reject work that does not contribute to a named objective or
required enabling system. Do not use it to authorize workload beyond constraints.

## Checklist

- [ ] Campaign dates span exactly 184 days.
- [ ] Mission is linked to evidence.
- [ ] Outcomes are not guaranteed.
- [ ] Constraints override schedule pressure.

## Decision Framework

Accept proposed work when it advances a chartered objective, produces verifiable
evidence, fits capacity, and respects constraints. Otherwise defer, reduce, or
reject it.

## Common Failure Modes

- Converting the mission into a rank or salary promise.
- Expanding scope after every new opportunity.
- Confusing activity volume with verified capability.

## Recovery Strategy

Return to the mission, list current work, and remove or defer items with no
traceable contribution.

## Examples

A tested FPGA artifact contributes to technical capability and portfolio
evidence. Collecting unrelated certificates without assessment does not.
