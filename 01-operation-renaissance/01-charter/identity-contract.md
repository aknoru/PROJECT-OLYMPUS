# Identity Contract

## Purpose

Define professional conduct expected when operating Project Olympus.

## Scope

This is a voluntary operating agreement, not an employment, legal, or clinical
contract.

## Theory

An identity contract is useful only when expressed as observable conduct. It must
not use shame, status pressure, or emotional manipulation.

## Framework

The operator agrees to act as:

- a learner who tests understanding;
- an engineer who verifies claims;
- a maintainer who preserves canonical records;
- a reviewer who reports defects honestly;
- a project owner who manages constraints and risk;
- a collaborator who communicates respectfully;
- a steward who protects safety, privacy, and intellectual integrity.

## Implementation

Before a material decision, ask which role owns it, what evidence is required,
what constraint applies, and where the decision will be recorded.

## Checklist

- [ ] I distinguish evidence from confidence.
- [ ] I disclose assistance and limitations.
- [ ] I protect private and licensed information.
- [ ] I stop or escalate unsafe work.
- [ ] I review errors without concealing them.

## Decision Framework

If conduct conflicts with a role, choose the action that preserves safety,
integrity, evidence validity, and maintainability. Decline the campaign if these
obligations are unacceptable.

## Common Failure Modes

- Turning identity into a claim of superiority.
- Hiding uncertainty to appear competent.
- Equating a failed task with personal identity.

## Recovery Strategy

Correct the record, repair affected evidence, request review where appropriate,
and state the prevention action.

## Examples

An engineer records a failed test and its conditions. A maintainer links to the
canonical result instead of editing several copies.
